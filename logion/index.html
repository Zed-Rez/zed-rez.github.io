Improving Logion: Weighted Levenshtein for Byzantine Greek Emendation

[source] · extends Princeton's Logion (Cowen-Breen, Brooks, Graziosi, Haubold; ACL ALP-2023)

Key Finding

A phonetic/visual-weighted Levenshtein neighborhood significantly improves detection of real attested textual variants. On 333 variant loci from the SBLGNT apparatus criticus, the weighted filter recovers variants in the top-5 at 42.3% versus baseline's 35.7% (paired McNemar p < 10−3; 28 loci recovered that baseline missed vs. 6 lost). Top-1 trends positive (16.5% vs. 14.4%, p=0.30).

This result is the strongest signal across three evaluations of increasing realism. On the paper's synthetic protocol (n=200), removing the weighted filter costs −4.0pp top-1 (McNemar one-tailed p=0.029). On a realistic scribal-class corruption protocol, removing it costs −4.0pp top-10 (p=0.039).

What Was Changed

Logion computes a chance-correction ratio (CCR) for each word: how probable is the transmitted reading versus the most probable near-spelling? Positions with low CCR are surfaced for expert review. Two changes were tested:

Weighted Levenshtein (the one that works). The original filter is binary: any vocabulary pair within classical Levenshtein distance ≤1 gets equal weight. In Byzantine Greek this is wrong — itacism (η/ι/υ/ει/οι all → /i/), visual confusions in minuscule script (γ/τ, λ/μ, β/ν, ρ/π), and post-vocalic β/υ drive most copying errors. The weighted filter assigns substitution costs from documented confusion classes (Allen, Vox Graeca; West, Textual Criticism; Reynolds & Wilson, Scribes & Scholars) and produces a continuous weight rather than a binary gate. An itacism swap like ταις/τοις (cost 0.35) receives ~3.7× the weight of an unrelated same-distance edit like τις/οις (cost 1.00).

Chance-fit adaptive temperature (verified inert). A per-position softmax temperature that flattens the distribution when the transmitted word is implausible. On the §4.1 protocol: exact tie with baseline at top-1 (McNemar p=1.00, discordant counts b=7, c=7).

Negative Findings

The combined system (all improvements on) underperforms baseline (−1.5pp top-1, −3.5pp top-5). This is because two components dilute the gain from weighted-Lev:

  • CF-temperature is inert (exact tie at top-1, p=1.00).
  • Extras (E1–E4) are actively harmful on the realistic scribal protocol: removing them lifts top-1 from 26.5% to 30.5% (McNemar p=0.021, b=1, c=9). The lectio-difficilior penalty (E1) likely causes this — it discounts suggestions more common than the transmitted form, which is exactly the pattern when an itacism slip moves from a rare to a frequent spelling.

The recommended configuration is therefore: weighted-Lev on, everything else off.

Out-of-Corpus Deployment

The system was run on 26 paragraphs (~2,100 words) of Photius's Bibliotheca (codices 70, 92, 161, 165, 213) — a work outside Logion's Psellus fine-tune corpus, with documented transmission ambiguity across two manuscript families (Marcianus gr. 450 and 451). The shortlist surfaces candidates concentrated in the itacism/visual edit region, but none align with documented apparatus loci — best read as positions where the model and transmitted text most diverge, not as a curated emendation set.

Caveats

  • Domain shift. The public Logion checkpoint is fine-tuned on Psellus (Byzantine, 11th c.). The SBLGNT evaluation uses Koine Greek (1st c. NT); the Photius deployment uses 9th c. Byzantine. Absolute accuracy is lower than the paper's headline 90.5% on in-domain Psellus, but relative deltas (baseline vs. recommended on the same loci) are still valid.
  • The §4.1 protocol structurally disadvantages the weighted filter. It samples corruptions uniformly from the 24-letter alphabet, which implicitly favours an algorithm treating every edit as equally likely — i.e. the binary filter. The weighted filter's design intent is to exploit the non-uniform distribution of real scribal errors. The SBLGNT evaluation, which tests against real attested variants, is the fairest test.
  • Variant ≠ corruption. Many SBLGNT variants are disagreements between defensible editorial readings, not scribal slips. The test measures whether Logion finds these loci unusual in context, which is weaker than asking whether they are errors.
  • The weight matrix is hand-tuned from textbook sources (Allen, West, Reynolds & Wilson). A learned phonetic prior from Byzantine palaeographic corpora is a natural extension — retraining with the weighted neighbourhood could yield stronger gains.
